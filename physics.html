Searching for the Missing Mechanism

The previous chapter ended with an unanswered question. In biology we observe both the population and the environmental mechanism that acts upon it. In cosmology we observe only one realized history of the universe. If branch histories are part of the quantum description, what physical process could determine how they contribute to what we ultimately observe?

This question lies at the heart of the mathematical work described in these essays. The goal is not to invent an arbitrary rule for favouring one history over another. Rather, it is to understand whether a proposed conditioning can consistently be interpreted as part of the physical theory itself.

Every scientist is free to propose a conditioning mechanism. The important question, however, is not whether the proposal produces interesting predictions. The important question is whether such a mechanism can consistently belong to the physical framework of quantum cosmology.

The mathematical notes accompanying these essays address precisely this issue through what we call a consistency audit. Rather than asking whether a proposal is attractive or useful, the audit asks whether it satisfies a sequence of increasingly stringent consistency requirements. Only proposals that pass all of them remain viable candidates for describing a physical process.

The first requirement is representability. The proposed conditioning must be expressible within the mathematical language of the theory. If it cannot even be formulated consistently, it cannot describe a physical process.

The second requirement is admissibility. Introducing the conditioning must remain compatible with the underlying structure and dynamics of quantum cosmology. A proposal that contradicts the theory cannot become part of it.

The third requirement is probability support. A physical conditioning must preserve a consistent probabilistic interpretation. Without this, the theory would lose its ability to make meaningful statistical predictions.

Passing this audit does not prove that nature actually performs the proposed conditioning. Science rarely works that way. Instead, the audit establishes something more modest but equally important: that the conditioning is compatible with everything already built into the theory and may therefore be regarded as a genuine candidate for a physical mechanism.

This changes the role of conditioning completely. It is no longer introduced merely because it produces interesting calculations. Instead, it becomes a hypothesis about how the universe itself may respond to its underlying branching structure. The next chapter explores the remarkable consequences that would follow if such a mechanism really exists.
