The essays in this website explore two research programmes that developed independently yet share a common conceptual theme. Both ask how finite observers obtain meaningful information about a larger physical world.

In the quantum essays this question leads to observable descriptions, interfaces and observational equivalence. In the gravitational essays it leads to boundary response, information flow and new ways of thinking about inertia.

These connections should not be understood as a claim that quantum theory and gravitation have been unified. Rather, they illustrate how similar conceptual questions can appear in very different mathematical settings.

Perhaps the most important lesson is methodological. Instead of beginning with the assumption that every physical quantity is directly accessible, these essays begin with the finite observer. Observable physics is then understood as the result of a relationship between the observer and the wider world.

Whether this viewpoint ultimately proves fruitful is for future research to decide. If nothing else, it encourages asking familiar questions from an unfamiliar angle.
