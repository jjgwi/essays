A boundary does not necessarily transmit every influence equally well. Experience tells us that every physical system has a limited capacity to respond. Some patterns pass through easily, while others are weakened or may leave almost no observable trace.

This suggests thinking of a boundary as an information filter rather than as a perfect window onto the surrounding universe. The observable response depends not only on what exists outside the boundary but also on which features the boundary can faithfully transmit.

From this perspective, inertia is connected with questions of information as well as dynamics. Which aspects of the external world influence local motion? Which are suppressed? And does the structure of the response reveal something fundamental about gravitation itself?

One intriguing possibility is that the dominant response reflects the same kind of symmetry that characterises gravitational waves. This idea remains speculative, but it provides a direction for further investigation rather than a finished theory.

The emphasis throughout is therefore on identifying promising physical questions. The mathematics suggests interesting patterns, while future work must determine how far those patterns extend.
