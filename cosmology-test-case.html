Cosmology as a Test Case

Home

The early universe provides an ideal setting in which to test ideas about observation. Unlike an ordinary laboratory, the universe cannot be prepared again under different conditions. We observe only one cosmic history, from one location, long after the events that shaped it.

Modern cosmology therefore depends not only on theories of the early universe but also on understanding which traces of that early history remain observable today. Different theoretical pictures may lead to the same observable sky, while apparently significant differences may leave no measurable imprint.

From the viewpoint developed in these essays, cosmology is not a separate subject but an especially demanding example of bounded observation. The question is always the same: which information about the larger physical description survives in the observations available to us?

Seen in this light, cosmology becomes a natural testing ground for the ideas of observable information and observational equivalence introduced in the previous essays.
