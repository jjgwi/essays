Why Understanding Matters

Home

These essays grew from curiosity rather than certainty. Physics advances because we ask questions whose answers are not yet known. Every generation inherits remarkable discoveries, but also deeper mysteries. Understanding is therefore never complete. It is an ongoing conversation between observation, mathematics and imagination.

“The most beautiful thing we can experience is the mysterious. It is the source of all true art and science.”
— Albert Einstein

Yet curiosity alone is not enough. Understanding also carries responsibility. Modern society depends increasingly on scientific knowledge, and decisions about our future require citizens who are able to think critically about the natural world.

“Our species needs, and deserves, a citizenry with minds wide awake and a basic understanding of how the world works.”
— Carl Sagan

These essays do not claim to settle the questions they explore. Their purpose is more modest, and perhaps more enduring: to invite curiosity, to make modern physics more accessible, and to encourage the habit of asking careful questions.

For me, science is less a collection of answers than a way of approaching the world. A well-formulated question often possesses a beauty of its own. Unlike individual answers, which are refined or replaced as knowledge advances, a profound question can inspire generations of inquiry. It reminds us that understanding is not something we possess once and for all, but something we pursue.
