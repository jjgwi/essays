Which Differences Remain Observable?

Home

Science is often described as the search for differences. We compare one experiment with another and ask whether nature behaves differently. Yet there is an equally important question: which differences can never be observed?

Imagine two complete descriptions of the universe. They may differ in many details, but if every experiment available to a bounded observer produces the same results, those differences have no operational meaning for that observer. From the observational point of view, the two descriptions are equivalent.

The essays in this section explore this idea without assuming that observable information is simply a reduced version of the complete description. Instead, they investigate how an observable point of view is selected and which global differences survive that selection.

This shift in emphasis is important. The central problem is not merely how to discard information, but how to identify the information that genuinely belongs to a given observer.

Once this viewpoint is adopted, questions that seem unrelated—from laboratory physics to cosmology—can be discussed within the same conceptual framework.
