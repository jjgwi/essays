Quantum Observation: What This Is About

Home

Quantum mechanics provides a mathematical description of physical systems, but an observer never has unrestricted access to that description. Real experiments use only particular observables. The information obtained therefore depends not only on the quantum state but also on the observables that are physically available.

The essays in this section explore this distinction. Rather than beginning with measurement or wave-function collapse, they begin with observable algebras and ask how an observable description emerges from a more complete one.

Interfaces are introduced later as one possible physical realization of this idea. They are not assumed from the outset but arise after deciding which observable structure is physically relevant.
