Boundary Response and Inertia: What This Is About

Home

For centuries inertia has been regarded as an intrinsic property of matter. Yet many physicists, from Mach to Einstein and Sciama, wondered whether it might instead reflect the influence of the surrounding universe.

The essays in this section revisit that question from a modern mathematical viewpoint. Instead of beginning with isolated particles, they consider finite spatial regions and their physical response at the boundary. This leads naturally to Dirichlet-to-Neumann ideas and to the possibility that inertial behaviour reflects information transmitted through a boundary.

The goal is not to replace established physics but to investigate whether this perspective reveals structures that remain hidden in the conventional formulation.
