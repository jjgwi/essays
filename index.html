Notes on Physics

Plain-language essays on how parts of a quantum world become separately controllable, how physical records are formed, and which features of the universe can ever reach an observer.

The essays begin with a simple question: what must nature provide before we can meaningfully speak of two separate laboratories? They then follow the same idea into cosmology, where a complete quantum description of the universe may contain far more information than any observer inside it can recover.

Essays

  • What this is about
    An introduction to the common theme: the physical structures that turn parts of a quantum world into controllable systems, lasting records, and observable facts.
  • Why interfaces?
    Why distance and causal separation are not always enough to make two regions independent in quantum field theory—and how a finite buffer between them can supply the missing structure.
  • Which differences are observable?
    Why two proposed quantum states of the universe can be mathematically different yet observationally identical, and how physical records decide which differences survive.
