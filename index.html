Notes on Physics

Plain-language essays on quantum interfaces, the information carried by physical records, and the limits of what a global quantum state can reveal to observers within the universe.

These essays are informal companions to technical documents 60 and 62. They follow the current order of the argument: first ask when an interface is required for independent physical operations; then ask how dynamics selects stable record-bearing observables; finally ask which distinctions between global quantum states survive in those observables.

Essays

  • What this is about
    An introduction to the shared programme: from commuting local domains, through response-selected records, to the underdetermination of a universal quantum state by internal observations.
  • Why interfaces?
    Why locality alone does not make two quantum-field-theoretic laboratories independently controllable, and how the split property supplies the missing operational structure.
  • Which differences are observable?
    How a realized interface restricts a global state, which differences are erased or merely suppressed, and how this can be audited in cosmological Gaussian models and competing boundary proposals.
